Overview

Does the highest-scoring job share the resume’s occupation?

MCA Second Review — controlled evaluation of the Review 1 resume–job matching approach.

Alignment with Review 1 Proposal

Alignment with Review 1 Proposal

  • Original proposal: NLP-based resume–job matching using TF-IDF/cosine similarity, skill extraction, skill gap analysis, and explainable insights.
  • Current work: Implements the lexical matching pipeline (80/10/10 TF-IDF + skill coverage blend), occupation-specific skill mining with hold-out, and per-match score breakdown (body, title, skill coverage) plus Has/Lacking skill lists.
  • Planned next: Semantic embeddings (e.g. Sentence-BERT), richer skill extraction, and a simple interactive dashboard.

Method

Method

  • Resumes from snehaanbhawal/resume-dataset (ground-truth occupation on each resume).
  • Human-written job postings from avinashmynampati/human-vs-fake-vs-ai-generated-job-descriptions; job occupation inferred from title keywords.
  • Ranking score (blend): 80% cosine TF-IDF on the job description, 10% on the job title, and 10% skill coverage (share of occupation skill terms on the job that also appear on the resume).
  • Skill terms: Mined from other resumes in the same occupation (unigrams/bigrams that are distinctive in-category). Resumes in the evaluation sample are held out so skill lists do not leak test text.
  • Evaluation proxy: Occupation match is used because no human relevance judgments are available—if the top job shares the resume’s occupation label, the ranker aligned with an checkable career-area signal.

Evaluation metrics

Evaluation metrics

Best match The rank-1 job’s occupation equals the resume’s occupation.
Found in the first 3 At least one of ranks 1 through 3 has the same occupation as the resume.
Mean reciprocal rank (MRR) One divided by the rank of the first correct occupation, averaged over resumes.

Closing

Limitations & next steps

Limitations

  • Small sample size (2–3 resumes per occupation)—metrics are illustrative, not statistically robust.
  • Job occupation inferred from title keywords; some errors are label noise.
  • Purely lexical matching today—no semantic embeddings in this build.

Next steps

  • Add semantic embeddings (e.g. Sentence-BERT / MiniLM) as a second ranking signal.
  • Improve skill extraction and occupation lexicons.
  • Build a simple interactive dashboard for upload-and-rank demos.

Bottom line: Controlled sample: blend 57.1% Best Match, MRR 0.701. Broader sample: blend 58.3% Best Match, MRR 0.683. The blend (80% description, 10% title, 10% skill coverage) delivers the reported rank and explainable skill-gap insights aligned with Review 1.
