What this build demonstrates

What is ready to show

  • Skill extraction: Distinctive occupation terms are mined from resumes that are not in the scored sample, then matched on resume and job text.
  • Ranking: Each pair gets a blend score — 80% job-description TF-IDF, 10% job-title TF-IDF, 10% skill coverage — plus three comparison methods (baseline TF-IDF, text only, skill coverage only).
  • Skill gaps: Each ranked job lists skills the resume already has and skills the job asks for that the resume lacks.
  • Left for the final review: A semantic embedding ranker, and an interactive page where a recruiter or candidate can paste a resume and a job.

Method

Method

  • Resumes from snehaanbhawal/resume-dataset, restricted to the information-technology category, then labeled software development, systems administration, or IT support from role phrases in the text.
  • Human-written job postings from avinashmynampati/human-vs-fake-vs-ai-generated-job-descriptions, restricted to IT titles, then labeled with the same three role families.
  • Ranking score (blend): 80% cosine TF-IDF on the job description, 10% on the job title, and 10% skill coverage (share of occupation skill terms on the job that also appear on the resume).
  • Skill terms: Mined from other resumes in the same occupation (unigrams/bigrams that are distinctive in-category). Resumes in the evaluation sample are held out so skill lists do not leak test text.
  • Evaluation proxy: A match is correct when the top job is in the same IT role family as the resume. HR, sales, finance, and other non-IT occupations are not in this sample.

Evaluation metrics

Evaluation metrics

Best match The rank-1 job’s occupation equals the resume’s occupation.
Found in the first 3 At least one of ranks 1 through 3 has the same occupation as the resume.
Mean reciprocal rank (MRR) One divided by the rank of the first correct occupation, averaged over resumes.

Closing

Limitations & next steps

Limitations

  • Small sample size (2–3 resumes per occupation)—metrics are illustrative, not statistically robust.
  • IT role labels come from phrases in the resume and the job title, so some errors are label noise.
  • Purely lexical matching today—no semantic embeddings in this build.

Next steps

  • Add semantic embeddings (e.g. Sentence-BERT / MiniLM) as a second ranking signal.
  • Improve skill extraction and occupation lexicons.
  • Build a simple interactive dashboard for upload-and-rank demos.

Bottom line: Controlled sample: blend 33.3% Best Match, MRR 0.639. Broader sample: blend 55.6% Best Match, MRR 0.726. The analytical core (extraction, ranking, scores, and skill gaps) is about 81% complete. Across all five promised outcomes, including a live app, the estimate is 69%. The blend (80% description, 10% title, 10% skill coverage) is the reported ranker.
